# Nest Learning 3rd gen local control and 24 V lockout research (2026-09-29)

Method: web search plus page fetches, ~24 calls. Several Google community pages redirect and could not be read directly; those points rest on search-result snippets and are marked. Nothing on the LAN was touched.

## Q1 bottom line
Local (no-cloud) control of a Nest Learning 3rd gen is, as far as public evidence shows, NOT available in 2026 by any supported or turnkey route. The only local-adjacent path is a 2021 secure-boot exploit claim for Gen 3 that has no known usable release. Confidence: high that no supported path exists (official docs plus absence in every project README); medium that no unsupported one exists (absence of evidence).

a. SDM API (cloud). Official: runs on Google servers, needs internet; one-time non-refundable US$5 Device Access registration; "All Google Nest thermostat models are supported", which includes the 3rd gen. Sources: https://developers.google.com/nest/device-access/supported-devices ; https://developers.google.com/nest/device-access/get-started ; https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/nest/ (all fetched/searched 2026-09-29; pages undated, current). Confidence: high (official doc).

b. Matter. Only the Nest Thermostat (2020) got Matter (2023 rollout; TechHive/iDownloadBlog Apr 2023). Nest Learning 4th gen (2024) ships with Matter (Google Store page; I did not verify local-Matter specifics). Learning 3rd gen: Google Community thread (Jan 2025 per snippet) says Google has "no news" on Matter for 3rd gen; no evidence anything changed by 2026. Could not open the thread itself (redirect), so this is from search snippet. Sources: https://www.googlenestcommunity.com/t5/Home-Automation/Matter-Support-for-3rd-gen-Nest-Learning-Thermostat/m-p/480844 ; https://www.techhive.com/article/1789782/googles-nest-thermostat-gets-matter-support.html . Confidence: medium-high no Matter on 3rd gen.

c. Google Home APIs (Android/iOS, 2024-25). The "Thermostat" device type in the Home APIs is defined in terms of Matter clusters; the docs say the APIs give low-latency local control of Matter devices. That helps Matter thermostats (2020 / 4th gen), not the 3rd gen, which is a non-Matter Works-with-Nest device reached via the cloud. No 3rd-gen local path found. Source: https://developers.home.google.com/apis/android/supported-device-types (fetched 2026-09-29, no per-model note). Confidence: medium (inference from Matter-only framing, no explicit statement about 3rd gen).

d. Community firmware. Google ended support for Gen 1 and Gen 2 in Oct 2025 (support blog; could not open, from search snippet). NoLongerEvil (codykociemba/NoLongerEvil-Thermostat, Nov 2025; 9to5Google 2025-11-03; Hackaday 2025-11-11) supports Gen 1 and Gen 2 ONLY, using the GTVHacker-derived OMAP DFU bootloader route; the 3rd gen is not supported. It does provide a self-hosted server (Home Assistant integration, MQTT/REST reported by secondary sources). Risks stated by the project: "Improper firmware can brick your device", low-level boot access, no responsibility. Flashing removes Google features and would void warranty (inference). Gen 3 uses an i.MX/HAB secure boot and different hardware, so the OMAP jailbreak does not apply. An Exploitee.rs forum post (2021-02-24) claims a HAB boot-ROM flaw giving root/SSH on Gen 3, "PoC coming soon"; no public release found. "FlyingNest" and "Cuckoo's Nest": I did not find sources for these in the calls made; unverified. Sources: https://github.com/codykociemba/NoLongerEvil-Thermostat ; https://9to5google.com/2025/11/03/google-nest-thermostat-old-revive-project/ ; https://hackaday.com/2025/11/11/nest-thermostat-now-100-less-evil/ ; https://forum.exploitee.rs/viewtopic.php?t=112843 . Confidence: high 3rd gen unsupported by NoLongerEvil; low on the Gen 3 exploit's usability.

e. LAN protocols. No source found showing anyone controlling a 3rd gen over Weave or another local port (searches for 9543/11095 returned nothing on Gen 3). Speculation only. Confidence: low (absence of evidence). Do not probe the device; the owner said no LAN access.

f. Offline behaviour. Multiple consumer sources (BGR; Nest Community; retailer guides) say the 3rd gen keeps working without internet: keeps its learned schedule, Eco/away logic uses its own sensors, ring and Quick View allow manual changes at the device; loses app, voice, remote access, notifications. Sources: https://www.bgr.com/2105399/can-you-use-google-thermostat-without-internet/ . Confidence: medium-high (secondary sources, consistent with how the device is known to work). Practical point: a person at home can always set it down by hand; but the schedule will resume its own setpoints and may re-call heat/cool after an outage, so a hardware lockout is the only unattended mechanism.

Overall: not "truly impossible" in principle (unreleased 2021 exploit), but there is no usable local path. Do not plan on one. The owner's reading (cloud only) is correct. Cloud control fails when the internet is down, so the hardware fallback in Q2 is the sound design.

## Q2

a. Powerwall 3 load-shed relay. Tesla's own Install Manual: from PW software 25.2 / Tesla One 12.32, a low-voltage dry-contact circuit controls a load; "an air conditioning load controlled by a thermostat can be wired to the Powerwall 3 Aux wiring terminals"; wiring interrupts one of the two low-voltage control wires between thermostat and condenser (the Y wire; Y energizes the contactor in both heat and cool per the search summary); closed on-grid, open off-grid; rated 60 V AC/DC, 2 A; config path Settings > Advanced Settings > TACO Load Control Relay > Off-grid Load Shedding; optional override switch; NOT approved for AC-coupled solar. Backup Gateway 2 installs can have a second circuit (one on PW3, one on BG2, up to two). The manual shows one AC unit, single load per circuit. Tesla gives no guidance on heat pumps, strip heat, or multiple units per contact (I found none). One contact driving two 24 VAC pilot relays: electrically plausible (coil draw of small 24 VAC relays is ~0.1-0.3 A, within 2 A; contact is 60 V rated so 24 VAC is fine), but that is my engineering inference, not a Tesla-endorsed configuration; an installer should confirm. Prefer wiring the two relays' coils in parallel and using the relay contacts in each unit's Y (and AUX) circuit, with a separate 24 VAC source for the coil so the load-shed contact is not in series with thermostat signal current. Alternative: use a Y-series contact only if the contact carries the thermostat's Y current (small). Fail-safe note: "closed on-grid, open off-grid" means the unit is locked out whenever the TACO is unpowered too (fail-safe for backup, but confirm behaviour if the TACO loses power on-grid). Sources: https://energylibrary.tesla.com/docs/Public/EnergyStorage/Powerwall/3/InstallManual/BackupGateway/2/en-us/GUID-570320A6-25C3-48F1-B96D-7CFB4E1FF573.html ; https://energylibrary.tesla.com/docs/Public/EnergyStorage/Powerwall/3/DeviceSetupGuide/en-us/GUID-C7B6F3FE-F71B-45CD-B5B4-AF946A2A9730.html ; Tesla Motors Club load-shed thread https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/powerwall-automated-load-shedding.192961/ (not read in detail). Confidence: high for the Tesla facts (official); low-medium for the pilot-relay scheme (inference).

b. Which wires. Nest terminals: Y1 compressor, W2/AUX aux (strip) heat, E emergency, G fan, O/B reversing valve (Nest install guide: http://support-assets.nest.com/images/pro-faq/Nest-Pro-Installer-Guide.pdf). Practice (my synthesis; no single source found stating this exact recipe): open Y1 (and Y2 if present) AND the aux/emergency heat call (W2/AUX/E), since strip heat is often the largest load (typically 5-15 kW; owner must confirm). Leave O/B alone (cutting it just changes valve state, no load benefit, and can confuse the Nest). G is optional: leaving it lets the blower run on a call (small load); cutting it prevents fan-only. Keep R and C intact so the Nest stays powered. Caveat: if the system uses a dedicated 24 V-fed strip-heat sequencer, the W2 interruption must be upstream of that. Also note the Nest and controllers often disable strip heat via outdoor-unit/air-handler lockout instead (see d). Confidence: medium (engineering practice, not a cited procedure).

c. Side effects. The Nest detects wiring at install/connect time and raises errors such as E103 (Y1 wire failure) if it sees a fault on Y1 (JustAnswer Q&A, undated: https://www.justanswer.com/hvac/dvrep-hvac-issue-getting-e103-y1-wire-failure-nest-3rd.html). An open relay in series with Y1 looks like a disconnected wire only if the Nest re-runs wire detection (on reboot, wire move, or after power loss); also a Nest that is calling and sees no equipment response may show "delayed" messages and Nest power-stealing via Y (trickle current) can make the contactor hum when Y is intermittently opened (per search results). Advice: since the Nest is stealing power via C/R, keep C connected. Test before relying on it: open the contact and observe the Nest for alerts. Not verified for the 3rd gen specifically. Short-cycle protection: many outdoor units or air handlers have built-in 3-5 minute anti-short-cycle timers (Goodman ASC-01A kit is 3 minutes; Trane min off 5 min per search snippet: https://highperformancehvac.com/delay-timers-air-conditioners/ ; https://www.trane.com/residential/en/resources/glossary/hvac-short-cycling/); this is model-dependent, so the owner must check the specific units. Nest also has its own compressor protection (minimum off timer, the "delayed" message: https://www.trunetto.com/troubleshooting/smart-thermostats/nest/nest-thermostat-delayed-message-wont-start-hvac). Confidence: medium.

d. DR/lockout terminals. Not confirmed for specific brands in this pass. A relevant real-world precedent: Mike Holt forum thread "Heat strip lock out on generator power" (403 blocked, not read; title only). Many heat pump air handlers and thermostat-side boards have an "outdoor thermostat / lockout" input or utility DR terminal, and separate Tesla-style generator-lockout relays exist. I did not find brand pages. Owner's model numbers are needed. Confidence: low.

e. WiFi relay. Shelly Plus 1 / Plus 1 UL have dry contacts, local HTTP/RPC, MQTT and webhooks without cloud (Shelly docs: https://shelly-api-docs.shelly.cloud ; retailer pages). Suitability: contact rating is for line voltage and is fine for 24 VAC low current; the Shelly Plus 1 UL is powered by 110-240 VAC or 12 V DC (5 V/12 V DC variants exist), NOT 24 VAC directly, so it cannot be powered from the air-handler transformer without a converter (I did not verify a 24 VAC-input variant; do not assume). Better: power from a 24 VAC to 12 V DC converter or a wall supply. Note that a Shelly on the household WiFi during an outage needs a WiFi router on backup (Powerwall-backed), and the Linux machine talks to it over LAN. Confidence: medium (retailer and vendor sources; power-input detail from my knowledge).

## Questions only the owner can answer
1. Does each air handler have electric strip heat, and how many kW? Is the Nest wired to W2/AUX/E?
2. Model numbers of outdoor unit and air handler (for DR/lockout terminals and anti-short-cycle timers).
3. Is the Powerwall 3 solar DC-coupled (load shedding is not approved for AC-coupled solar), and is a Backup Gateway 2 present (second circuit)?
4. Which Nest terminals are actually connected (Y1, Y2, O/B, G, C)? Photo of the Nest wire labels.
5. Should the lockout also apply when the grid is up but the battery is low (needs the Linux/Shelly route, since the TACO only follows grid state)?
6. Which Nest 3rd gen model and software version (for any future Matter announcement).
